A gate holds its bound

The gate never admitted more downloads than its bound: in all 7 recorded runs, with bounds from 1 to 24, the most downloads in flight equalled the bound.

  • A resource gate on simulated downloads
  • Losses shown

recorded runs where the most downloads in flight equalled the gate bound
7 of 7

What it does not do

Every loss in this record, stated once
  • The gate does not choose its bound for you. A bound set too low serialises the work, and a bound set too high lets a crowded service slow down. Finding the bound is what the tuner is for.

How it was measured

Stated once, for every figure on this page
Workload
24 jobs are queued at once through a ForEach. Each passes a Network gate and awaits a simulated download of 40 ms. The simulated service stretches the wait once more than 6 downloads are in flight. 7 fixed gate bounds from 1 to 24, one recorded run each, after a warm-up run.
Engines
EvalApp pipeline with Gate and ForEach
Check
Every job records its own timestamps, and the number of downloads in flight is counted inside the gated step, so the bound is checked against what actually ran.
Statistic
One recording per bound.
Memory and logging
Nothing is retained between runs.

Limits

  • The work is a simulated wait, not a real network. It shows that the gate and its queue hold the bound, not how any real service behaves.
  • It checks the gate and does not compare EvalApp with another library.
