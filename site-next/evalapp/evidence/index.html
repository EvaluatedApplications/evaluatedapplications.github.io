What a gate bound does

The gate never admitted more downloads than its bound: in every recorded run the most in flight equalled the bound. The time to finish 24 queued jobs fell from 1,113 ms at a bound of 1 to 139 ms at 8, then rose again to 410 ms when all 24 ran at once, because the simulated service slows when it is crowded.

Where it is slower

Every loss in this record, stated once
  • A bound of 1 took 1,113 ms, 8.0 times the best bound. The gate does not choose its bound for you, and a bound set too low is the slowest result here.
  • A bound of 24, the whole batch at once, took 410 ms, 2.9 times the best bound. A higher bound is not faster when the thing behind the gate slows under load.

How it was measured

Stated once, for every figure on this page
Workload
24 jobs are queued at once through a ForEach. Each passes a Network gate and awaits a simulated download of 40 ms. The simulated service stretches the wait once more than 6 downloads are in flight. 7 fixed gate bounds from 1 to 24, one recorded run each, after a warm-up run.
Engines
EvalApp pipeline with Gate and ForEach
Check
Every job records its own timestamps, and the number of downloads in flight is counted inside the gated step, so the bound is checked against what actually ran.
Statistic
One recording per bound, in milliseconds. A repeated recording varies by a few percent.
Memory and logging
Nothing is retained between runs.

Limits

  • The work is a simulated wait, not a real network. It shows how the gate and its queue behave, not the speed of any real service.
  • The best bound here is a property of the simulated service, which slows past 6 in flight. A real service has its own shape, which is what the tuner exists to find.
  • It measures the gate and does not compare EvalApp with another library.
