Fifty reads, one store

One fixed-size array of exact integers. Counts, order, parity, time and nearest words, each in a single pass. When it is full, it refuses instead of guessing.

Finding

Fifty reads, one store. One fixed-size array of exact integers answers fifty different questions, each in a single pass over the array. The one exception is CausalReads, which takes one read per prefix. Counts by key. Counts by flag word. The nearest stored words. Arrangement order. Parity. Time. Joint counts across any chosen faces. Every count it returns is exact within the field's range (about 1.15 x 10^18 on the default field), and when the store is full the key views refuse rather than guess.

In brief

The Pleroma is a new construction and a mathematical discovery. Its proof is the code.

The numbers were measured against the standard. At 2,064 and 2,580 bits, the two budgets the benchmark ran, the Pleroma recovered 64 items exactly, in 200 of 200 trials. Plate's holographic memory (HRR), and its phase form (FHRR), with the best decoder tried, recovered 4 items reliably and none from 16 upward. Past the Pleroma's capacity, 65 to 96 items, it refused all 12,800 trials and gave no wrong answer. The baselines answered every time, and every answer was wrong.

How it was found

The project starts where its philosophy and ethics start, and reduces to the fewest axioms that reasoning needs. In its own words:

It is how we start from philosophy and ethics to figure out the most minimal set of axioms for reasoning, and build up from that on first principles.

Three of its statements carry the reduction: "Consciousness exists.", "Non-contradiction is necessary for coherent reasoning.", and "Claims about reality are more reliable when tested against reality."

Two axioms follow: there is an observer, and a resolved state does not contain both P and not-P. From them comes the first distinction. Whatever appears from the void comes with its opposite, so 0 = (+1) + (−1). The project calls this pair the syzygy.

Once the syzygy is in hand, its symmetries can be counted. They are the signed permutations of the cross-polytope, and they split exactly into two parts: sign flips, each its own inverse, and permutations. The flips form the ouroboros, and the permutations form the monad.

The project's rule for climbing from here: "A tick therefore adds one symmetry, and climbing the ladder is mapping the lineage of symmetries in order."

The thesis comes next. The project states it as: "The Pleroma ... is the one object all their holograms are shadows of." A shadow is what the larger object shows when it is read on some of its faces and not others.

The search for that object gives a map: the ladder laid out as rungs, each read across three faces. The map is predictive. It says what each cell should be, and each cell is then built and proved.

The method is the same for every cell. The prediction and its null are written first. The operation is built as a public member of the store. Its answer is checked against a brute-force rebuild, and the test is broken on purpose to confirm it fails for the right reason. For rung 3, ten symbols bound as four-symbol sequences give 715 distinct multisets, 10,000 distinct orders, and 256 parity classes, each checked against the rebuilt store.

The rungs said which operations superpose

The derivation's operator rule: a symmetry is a group element, and a weighted sum of group elements is one kernel. It acts on each harmonic with one multiplication, so its cost is one pass whatever the size of the sum. The same rule says choosing is not linear, so the one choice is made at the read.

The code was measured against that rule across the public surface. Eight of the 41 operators that change the store join one kernel and apply in one pass; key shifts do so on a keyed kernel. Sixteen take one pass per term in a mix. Seventeen are writes that are not kernel elements. The 50 reads are separate, each one pass, and nine of them are answered through a read set.

On a 10,464-cell store, Apply takes 0.17 to 0.44 ms per call, for a kernel of anything from 1 operator up to 10,368, with no trend across that range. Building the same store from the moved items takes 1,916 ms at 1,000 operators. The figures are a Release build on a loaded machine, two runs.

Protection and size

Each face protects its own store in its own way.

  • Counts: a page on a larger cycle, with r keys known to be empty, rebuilds any r lost harmonics exactly: 300 of 300 at each erasure size from 1 to 5. It detects corrupted harmonics and corrects up to half that many at unknown positions.
  • Choices: a second prime holds a mirror of every cell. One corrupted cell in either copy is detected, 10,000 of 10,000.
  • Parities: a parity page, the sum of the pages, rebuilds any one lost page, 100 of 100.

The unit workload is 53,031 pages of 110 cells each, and it fits in 49.6 MB. A plane of 14 flag bits and 16,386 cells stores at 8.02 bytes per cell. A sparse page holding one key takes 183 bytes and reads in 0.4 µs.

Built on

The Pleroma is a new construction. Each brick it stands on is established mathematics or an established algorithm, credited here:

  • Plate's Holographic Reduced Representations (1995), and its phase form FHRR. The continuous component is the bundle, and both are the equal-storage baselines.
  • Reed and Solomon (1960), with Bose and Ray-Chaudhuri (1960): syndrome decoding of power sums, which the key view uses.
  • Berlekamp (1968) and Massey (1969): the Berlekamp-Massey recurrence, with a re-encode check.
  • PinSketch (Talbot et al., 2012) and minisketch (Poinsot et al., 2017): exact set sketches that subtract and decode the difference.
  • The Walsh-Hadamard transform: counts under any predicate of the flag word, in one read.
  • Fourier analysis on finite groups: binding as convolution.
  • The Chinese remainder theorem: joining coprime key cycles.
  • Hamming codes (1950): syndromes that locate a single error.
  • Galois linear feedback shift registers and the Frobenius map on GF(2^m).
  • Time diffusion on a cycle, from Markov chains.
  • Krawtchouk polynomials, which underlie the views.
  • The universal coefficient theorem, Schläfli's classification of regular polytopes, Galois and Abel-Ruffini on the simplicity of A_5, Kolmogorov's criterion for circulation, Bott periodicity, and the Altland-Zirnbauer and Kitaev symmetry classes.

Where the code does not superpose today

  • Key scaling costs one pass per term in a mix. A key shift joins a kernel only on a keyed kernel; on an unkeyed one a mix of key shifts costs one pass per term.
  • Right relabelling, conjugation and flag sign flips cost one pass per term.
  • Bit maps on the flag cube, and fixed per-key or per-tick weights, are not kernel elements. A mix of them costs one pass per term.
  • Commuting with the symmetry is necessary for a kernel and not sufficient. Hadamard commutes and is not a kernel element, and ShiftKeys is one only through a keyed kernel.
  • Each read makes its own transform by default. A transform is shared between reads only inside an internal planner that is not exposed.
  • A kernel on a store with a check prime runs through a mirror kernel in the check prime, built from the same elements; a kernel built from a shape without that check prime is refused.
  • Key reads refuse past their capacity. A store keeps 2K+1 harmonics per key line, so K+1 distinct keys always refuse. A complete key band goes to 4,096 keys.
  • Counts are exact within the field's signed range. A plain read does not check for a wrap. On a store with a check prime, VerifyCheck detects one within the check bound.
  • The store runs on the CPU. Its gain is exactness and many views on one array, not raw speed for a single read.
  • Rung 8's contradiction location is not built, and reunification, the rung where observers merge, is open.

Go deeper: the four families and the order face

The store holds the harmonic transform of its items on one product of four families: the key cycle, the flag cube, the symmetric group and the time cycle. Each view reads its own slice of the same cells, because each view is a shadow on some of the faces.

The search found the flags and the permutations as the two halves of the root's symmetry. The key cycle is the syzygy's own cycle, realised on a finite field. The time family is the rung-7 step repeated. A direct product does not depend on the order of its factors. The listing follows the order in which the search met them.

The order face is built for S_2 to S_5, up to 120 arrangements. The shape definition sets the cap, and the record gives no further reason for it.

Five is also where repeated splitting first meets a part it cannot split: the alternating group A_5, of order 60, the rotation group of the icosahedron. That is a coincidence of the two facts, and the cap is not derived from it.

The splitting chains are: 2 to 1; 6 to 3 to 1; 24 to 12 to 4 to 1; 120 to 60 to 60. The shadow of the root holds on sequences, 10,000 of 10,000, and on loops, 9,000 of 9,000.

Go deeper: signed removal and plus and minus

Removal adds the opposite count. This fits the project's rule that a distinction, once made, is not unmade. The cell layout keeps its size, and a removed item leaves its cells at zero.

The cancellation test adds an item and then its opposite, and every cell is zero afterwards, with the default field and with three primes.

A plus and a minus of the same item share one cell and cancel there exactly.

Go deeper: closeness on the cube

Closeness on the flag cube is Hamming distance. Near(query, r) counts the stored items whose flag word lies within r bits of the query, and Nearest returns the nearest stored words with their distances. Both are exact at 200 keys. The key view refuses past its capacity, but the flag-cube read does not depend on the key count.

Go deeper: the lid

Sample is a read-time draw. It returns a value with probability count over total, taken from the exact counts, and it leaves the store unchanged. A check confirms that every draw lands on its exact share.

The map places the lid at the top of the ladder: the top rung iterated, with the faces divergence, forgetting and pseudo-randomness.

  • A complete cycle sums to exactly zero, and a symbol read on coprime cycles decodes exactly, 20,000 of 20,000.

Go deeper: the gaps, grouped

The theory says these should superpose, and the code does not:

  • Key shifts and key scaling permute the key harmonics. A keyed kernel has the key-harmonic axis, so a key shift is one of its elements; key scaling is not.
  • Right relabelling and conjugation act from the right on the order face. The kernel holds the left action only. A superposed conjugation exists in an experiment record and not in the library.
  • Flag sign flips and per-key or per-tick weights cost one pass per term.
  • Relation faces under projection and under the Chinese remainder theorem.
  • Causal reads take one read per prefix. A prefix structure over the sequence is not built.

Design limits, set by the data rather than a missing operation:

  • A complete key band costs one cell per key, up to 4,096 keys. A truncated band holds 2K+1 harmonics per key line.
  • Exact keys carry no metric, so a nearest-key read needs the continuous component, which is approximate.
  • Choosing (argmax, sampling, a decode that refuses) happens once, at the read.

Other boundaries:

  • Exact within the signed range. A plain read does not check for a wrap; VerifyCheck does on a store with a check prime, within the check bound.
  • Bind is refused with several primes, and ShiftKeys on several primes needs an explicit key cycle.
  • CombineCycles refuses a store with a relation face.
  • The joint slice is capped at 2^24 cells.
  • Tenants as flag bits share one store while tenant bits plus data bits stay at 16 or fewer.
