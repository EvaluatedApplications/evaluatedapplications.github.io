Game

Send your soldiers down the lanes and take their castle.

The battle never stops: every soldier is its own body, walking its lane and fighting what it meets. Every 30 seconds it pauses, and you have 30 seconds to choose what to send and how to order it.

Play DoctrineHow it plays

One player, against a computer opponent. It runs in your browser tab. Laid out for a phone screen: one column, 44 px tap targets.

How a match goes

Two castles face each other across three lanes. The battle runs for 30 seconds, then pauses. While it is paused you can see the whole board: every soldier, every lane, both castles. You spend a small budget of points on packs of soldiers, place each pack in a lane, and give a lane an order. Press Ready, or let the 30 seconds run out, and the next 30 seconds of battle play.

Destroy the enemy castle to win. If neither castle has fallen after 20 phases, it is a draw. The same seed raises the same match, so you can play it again and try something different.

The soldiers

Eight kinds of soldier can be sent: Spearmen, Shieldwall, Riders, Archers, Priests, Wights, Wolves and Catapults. Each has its own strength, speed and reach, and each stands better against some kinds than others. Spearmen stand against riders, riders ride down archers, archers shoot through shieldwall, and shieldwall holds against spearmen. Priests answer the cursed. Look at what the other side has sent before you choose.

Orders

A lane can be given one order: Advance, Hold, Fall back, Hunt or Guard. A lane told to hold digs in, and a line that holds its ground without being hit for a few ticks strikes harder until it is hit or moves. Your orders land together with the other side's at the end of the pause.

The battle

The ground is a baked Tracer navigation mesh, with walls that block sight and cover that protects. Every soldier is a separate agent that finds its path, takes cover and strikes on it, so a crowd really does funnel through a gap.

A match is a set of choices played out by a fixed set of rules. Two players who make the same choices in the same order get the same match, every time. That is also how a match can be checked: each pause carries a short fingerprint of the whole board.

What powers it

Each side's soldiers are items in a Pleroma store from the Phasor package. The battle runs on Tracer, and each step of the battle runs as an EvalApp pipeline. Nothing is uploaded: the whole game runs in your browser tab. Read more on the Pleroma page.

Ready when you are

Press play. The game loads once in your browser tab, and nothing needs installing.
