Game

Raise a general that learns how you command.

Choose from a muster of companies and a council of eight orders, pick four, and watch the battle. The general learns from every doctrine you set, in your browser, with nothing pre-trained, and suggests the next one.

Play DoctrineHow it plays

One player, against armies the game raises. It runs in your browser tab. Laid out for a phone screen: one column, 44 px tap targets.

How a game goes

A game is 3, 5 or 8 rounds. You start with an army levied for you and some gold. Each round you take your pick of the muster, choose four orders from the council, and watch one battle against an army the game raises.

The same seed raises the same enemies on the same ground, so you can replay a game. Leave the seed empty for a new one.

Orders

A doctrine is up to four orders, and no orders is allowed: every line then advances. There are two kinds. Army orders change the army itself before the battle. Field orders go to a whole line during it. An order the army says is not legal right now is greyed out.

Reform the ranks
Every cohort steps along the line, front to middle to back, then round. Each rank is a mix of kinds, so the three arrangements are a real choice.
Reforge
Cohorts that wore a trait shed it and the rest take it up. It costs gold for the soldiers who gain power.
Drill the host
Every cohort gains a year of seasoning and fights harder; after the ninth year the ring starts again.
Swear into
The soldiers of one kind join the kind before it.
Raise the banner
Cohorts that carry a trait fight harder this round.
Field orders
Advance, Hold, Flank, Fall back, Guard and Hunt. A line with no order advances.

The battle

The ground is a baked Tracer navigation mesh. Each cohort is a group of agents that path, take cover and strike on it, and you watch the arena as it runs.

A battle is simulated once, on your device, and recorded. The replay is a recording of that one battle: playing it back does not run it again, and you can scrub through it.

The general

Your general starts empty. It learns how you set a doctrine from your own choices, in your browser, with nothing pre-trained, so different players end up with different generals. It suggests and you decide.

Every doctrine you set is an example for it, and so is each battle result. Inside it are a small habit table and a tiny HoloFormer from the AlgFormer package. For each of the four slots the table speaks, unless the HoloFormer has been right on that slot at least two more times than the table over your recent choices, in which case it takes the slot.

The match record beside a suggestion is a score out of 1000 for how well its whole suggestion has matched what you then chose, over your recent choices. It starts low and climbs as the matches keep coming. Nothing is saved: refresh the page and the general is gone, though it carries what it has seen into your next game on the same page.

Army codes

At the end of a game you can copy an army code, a short piece of text that holds your army. Paste it on the start screen to play on with that army. It holds the army only: your general is not in it.

What powers it

Each cohort is an item in a Pleroma store from the Phasor package: its kind is the key, its traits are the flags, its place in the line is the arrangement, and its veterancy is a position on a ring of nine. An order is one operation on that store, and the list of orders you may give is read from the store.

The battle runs on Tracer, the rounds run as EvalApp pipelines, and the general uses AlgFormer. Nothing is uploaded: the whole game runs in your browser tab. Read more on the Pleroma page.

Ready when you are

Press play. The game loads once in your browser tab, and nothing needs installing.
