# Doctrine core loop v0 (coordinator as designer, 2026-10-21)

User verdict after playing: "it plays like crap, it feels like a sim, not a game; simplify to the core fun loop first, then
expand." Diagnosis (mine): the player operates a simulation (muster of 6, council of 8, four ordered slots, three formations,
rerolls, standards, a map, set and siege) and then watches an opaque fight. There is no moment where a choice visibly pays off.
The game is all setup and no payoff, and every system asks for attention before the first fun thing happens.

## The fun, in one sentence

Pick one of three cards, watch your army fight, and see that card matter; then do it again against something bigger until you lose.

## The loop (a run)

1. SEE THE ENEMY: a plain card of the next fight: their army as big icons and counts, and one line naming the threat
   ("Riders: they will charge your archers").
2. PICK ONE OF THREE: three cards, large and plain. A card is either a COMPANY (a unit pack: "6 Archers") or a TRICK (a passive
   that changes how your army fights: "Archers shoot from the back rank", "Spearmen brace against riders", "Priests bless
   the front"). No gold, no slots, no ordering, no formations. One tap. Cards that fit what you already own glow (visible
   synergy), and the card says what it will do in ten words.
3. WATCH A SHORT FIGHT: 15 to 25 seconds, big units, damage numbers, deaths, a clear moment when a trick fires ("Brace!" when
   the spears stop the charge). Speed buttons and skip. The chronicle becomes a few callout lines, not a log.
4. WIN: pick one of three rewards (same card pool); the enemy for the next fight is stronger. LOSE: the run ends with a score
   (fights won) and an instant "Again" (the same seed or a new one).
5. EVERY FIFTH FIGHT is a boss with a named threat and a better reward.

Three lives are cut to ONE: a lost fight ends the run (roguelike). The run is short (5 to 12 minutes) and instantly restartable.

## What is cut from the default experience (kept in the code, hidden)

The council of 8 and the four ordered slots, formations and Reform the ranks, rerolls, gold and the muster of 6, standards, the
overmap, set and siege as player rules (they stay as battle mechanics where they help a card, not as things to manage), the
unlock ladder, the general panel. Existing modes (3/5/8 and the war) stay reachable under 'More modes'. Nothing is deleted.

## Systems that make the picks matter (must be visible, not hidden numbers)

- A small set of ARCHETYPES the cards build toward (for example Volley: archers in the back and a wall in front; Charge: riders
  and wolves with a banner; Shieldwall: heavy front with priests; Horde: many cheap bodies). Three or four, each with a
  cheap, readable payoff the fight shows.
- Counters are the enemy's threats (the ring already exists: Plain beats Swift beats Ranged beats Armoured beats Plain), so
  the enemy card tells you which archetype to lean toward and the pick is a real decision.
- The general stays, but as a quiet suggestion highlight on the cards (its pick glows), never a panel to read.

## Build order

A. Quick Run mode as the new default start (showroom-owner, using the existing offers, packs and orders: three cards from the
   existing offer pools, tricks mapped to existing orders applied automatically, one life, enemy card, short fight, reward pick,
   score and Again). Report which Doctrine rules it needs (offer sizes, automatic orders, boss scaling, battle speed).
B. Doctrine rule tweaks for the loop (tracer-owner): card pools, trick set, enemy scaling, boss, short battle tick limit.
C. Juice: unit art (Kenney CC0), damage numbers, a charge and a brace moment.
D. Expand only after the loop is fun: bring back one system at a time (formations, then the map).

## How we know it works

A new visitor reaches the first fight in under 15 seconds, understands every card without reading a manual, can say why they
lost, and presses Again. Nothing is measured on that yet, since there are no players: the owner plays it and says.
